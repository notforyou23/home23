# Shakedown Feature Forge

This skill memorializes the successful pre-rewrite process used to make **“The Mandolin Knows”** from “Rubin and Cherise” on August 10, 2026.

Its product is a complete, sourced feature with a real governing idea. It is not a dossier, topic card, pipeline state transition, publishing operation, or jtr-voice rewrite.

## The central rule

**Find the sentence that makes the whole piece legible, then try to prove it wrong.**

For “Rubin and Cherise,” that sentence was:

> Reuben can lie, but his mandolin cannot.

That was not a decorative headline added after research. It selected the research, organized the evidence, and became the article’s spine. The second pressure point—this deeply Garcia song barely became a Grateful Dead song—supplied the historical counterweight.

Do not begin by filling a template. Do not begin with the pipeline. Begin by reading the thing closely enough to have a take.

## When to use

Use this skill when jtr names a Garcia, Hunter, Grateful Dead, JGB, song, performance, object, collaborator, or episode and wants the actual Shakedown story found and written.

Especially use it when he says:
- “straight up”
- “just do the piece”
- “find the story”
- “give it the Rubin and Cherise treatment”
- the pipeline is unfinished or would distract from the editorial work

Do not use it for:
- publishing or distribution
- a routine factual lookup
- a voice-only rewrite
- backlog selection
- North Star gap-filling
- forcing a weak subject into an article

## Actions

### `forge`

Run `forge` as one editorial workflow:

1. close-read the primary object
2. state a provisional governing idea
3. derive bounded research threads that could prove or break it
4. gather and validate authoritative sources claim by claim
5. follow meaningful discrepancies and variants
6. build an argument-shaped narrative movement
7. draft the complete sourced feature
8. run the adversarial editorial pass
9. freeze the pre-voice manuscript

The action ends at a verified manuscript. Publishing and voice rewriting are separate actions.

## Inputs

Minimum:
- one named subject
- any live hunch or unresolved tension already present in the conversation

Optional:
- local Garcia corpus references
- prior brain findings
- an existing draft
- a specific performance, lyric, interview, or object that triggered interest

## Method

### 1. Suspend machinery that is not helping

When the pipeline is unsettled or starts becoming the story, set it aside. Do not create assignments, cards, dossiers, handoffs, or premature artifacts merely because those surfaces exist.

Keep the subject in the foreground.

### 2. Perform a close read before broad research

Read the primary object first: lyrics, recording notes, setlist, interview, artifact, or performance evidence.

Write a provisional governing idea in one or two plain sentences. It must identify a mechanism or tension, not merely declare importance.

Strong:
- “Reuben can lie, but his mandolin cannot.”
- “The song’s deepest drama is a musician being exposed by his own instrument.”
- “One of Garcia’s most native imaginative worlds barely became a Dead song.”

Weak:
- “This is an underrated masterpiece.”
- “The song has a rich history.”
- “Garcia was a creative R&D system.”

The governing idea is provisional. Research must be allowed to break it.

### 3. Turn the hunch into bounded research threads

List the few questions that would prove, complicate, or kill the reading. For the originating feature, the live threads were:

1. the mandolin exposing Reuben’s betrayal
2. Ruby Claire as Cherise’s double
3. Hunter’s alternate verses and the underworld ending
4. the Orpheus / *Black Orpheus* lineage
5. the discarded first melody and second composition
6. title and spelling variants: Rubin/Reuben and Cherise/Cérise
7. JGB versus Grateful Dead performance chronology
8. why a deeply Garcia song remained mostly outside the Dead

Do not research “everything about” the subject. Pursue threads with editorial consequence.

### 4. Check existing knowledge without becoming captive to it

Search in this order:

1. local Shakedown registry and existing work, to avoid duplication
2. local Garcia corpus and provenance files
3. Jerry’s brain for prior findings and unresolved threads
4. existing COSMO research brains
5. the live web for primary and authoritative sources

Existing synthesis is a lead, never proof. Failed searches, unavailable brains, or broken subagents do not justify stopping if direct authoritative research remains possible.

Never read or cite the private Garcia corpus at `cosmo-content/_private/`.

### 5. Build a source ladder

Prefer the closest authority for each claim rather than one source for the entire article.

Typical ladder:

1. **Primary / official:** official lyrics, album notes, Garcia or Hunter interviews, official archive pages
2. **Authoritative chronology:** Jerrybase, official show pages, Dead.net
3. **Specialist documentary source:** well-maintained lyric/song histories that expose variants and point to original interviews
4. **Secondary commentary:** useful for leads only unless independently verified
5. **Forums/social posts:** discovery only

For the originating feature, the load-bearing source functions were:
- Jerry Garcia official song archive: credits, lyrics, release and performance summary
- official *Cats Under the Stars* page: album and composition context
- Grateful Dead Lyric and Song Finder: Hunter’s extra verses, spelling variants, and the 1979 WMMR quotation
- Jerrybase: exact performance counts and chronology
- Dead.net: verification of the final Grateful Dead performance

### 6. Follow differences, not just confirmations

The best story often appears where sources do not quite match.

Actively compare:
- official lyrics versus Hunter’s performed or published lyrics
- album title versus lyric-book spelling
- Garcia’s solo repertoire versus Dead repertoire
- remembered chronology versus database chronology
- mythic source versus American setting

In the originating feature, Hunter’s omitted Charon verses transformed an evocative ending into an explicit Orpheus story. The title variant “Cérise” made the red doubling visible. The four Dead performances, compared with Garcia’s much longer history with the song, created the historical tension.

Treat contradictions as doors. Resolve them or name them; never sand them flat.

### 7. Keep a claim-to-source ledger

Before drafting, every material factual claim needs a source assignment.

Track at minimum:
- claim
- source URL or absolute path
- source type
- exact support found
- confidence
- any unresolved conflict

Separate three kinds of sentence:

- **fact:** must be sourced
- **interpretation:** must be anchored in quoted or accurately described evidence
- **inference:** must be clearly presented as a reading, not smuggled in as biography or motive

Never invent motives, chronology, quotations, backstage scenes, or first-person experience.

### 8. Find the article’s movement

Do not dump the ledger into prose. Arrange the verified material as a discovery.

The successful movement for “The Mandolin Knows” was:

1. Cherise senses the betrayal
2. the mandolin reveals what Reuben denies
3. Ruby and Cherise emerge as doubles
4. Hunter’s missing verses open the underworld
5. Garcia names Orpheus and *Black Orpheus*
6. the song itself is revealed as having been musically reborn
7. performance history shows it was Jerry’s song more than the Dead’s
8. Reuben’s oath finally comes due

Each section should deepen or alter the governing idea. If a fact does neither, cut it.

### 9. Draft straight

Write the complete feature before applying a voice system.

Draft rules:
- open inside the song or event, not with throat-clearing
- state the governing idea early
- quote only the lines that do analytical work
- alternate close reading with verified history
- let the reader discover the larger pattern in stages
- use section headings only when the argument genuinely turns
- end by returning to the governing mechanism at a deeper level
- include a compact source list with each source’s role

Do not force the Shakedown SET I / SET II structure onto a piece that has found a better native shape. Do not pad to a target word count. Do not insert generic “Garcia as innovation system” doctrine unless the subject itself proves it.

### 10. Run an adversarial editorial pass

Before calling the feature complete, ask:

- Does the governing sentence still explain the whole piece?
- Which claim would a knowledgeable Deadhead challenge first?
- Did I confuse a lyric interpretation with a historical fact?
- Did a title, count, date, venue, final performance, or quotation drift between sources?
- Did I overclaim causation or motive?
- Is every section doing new work?
- Did machinery, process, or research narration enter the article?

Fix the source or weaken the claim. Never bluff.

### 11. Freeze the pre-voice manuscript

The complete sourced feature is its own artifact and checkpoint. Preserve it before any voice rewrite.

Only after this point may the Shakedown jtr voicepack run as a separate downstream action:
- task: `rewrite`
- register: `manuscript`

The voice pass may alter rhythm, diction, contractions, and intimacy. It must not originate research, change facts, add unsupported first-person claims, or replace the governing idea.

## Output contract

Return or write:

1. title
2. optional deck
3. complete feature
4. sources with the function each served
5. a brief verification note only when needed outside the manuscript

A successful output has:
- one memorable governing idea
- at least one meaningful tension or transformation
- authoritative support for material facts
- exact chronology where chronology matters
- interpretation anchored in the primary object
- no pipeline chatter in the prose
- no voicepack applied unless separately requested

## Gotchas

- A clever governing sentence is a hypothesis, not permission to cherry-pick.
- Lyrics can support interpretation but not unsupported claims about Hunter’s or Garcia’s private intent.
- Performance databases are authoritative for chronology, not for explaining why a song was or was not played.
- A specialist lyric site can expose variants and interview leads; attribute it and verify consequential claims where possible.
- “First,” “last,” “only,” and exact performance counts require an explicit chronology source.
- Do not let source accumulation replace judgment. Ten facts without a governing idea are still not a feature.
- Do not let the governing idea become a slogan repeated in every section.
- Do not apply the jtr voicepack before the sourced manuscript is frozen.
- Do not leak research process, tool failure, pipeline state, or editorial machinery into the feature.

## Examples

### Example input: song

```text
Subject: Rubin and Cherise
Live hunch: the mandolin exposes Reuben before he confesses, and the song feels more central to Garcia than its four Dead performances suggest.
Output: a complete sourced Shakedown feature; no pipeline actions and no voice rewrite yet.
```

Expected research threads: lyric mechanism, Hunter variants, mythic lineage, composition history, title variants, JGB/Dead chronology.

### Example input: instrument

```text
Subject: Garcia’s Travis Bean period
Live hunch: the aluminum-neck guitars were not a quirky detour but a bounded experiment between known systems.
Output: find the one argument the evidence can actually support, then write the feature straight.
```

Expected behavior: test the hunch against instrument dates, builders’ accounts, recordings, and performance chronology; abandon or narrow it if the evidence does not support the stronger claim.

### Example input: no story yet

```text
Subject: one unusual 1982 setlist
Live hunch: none yet.
Output: investigate whether a consequential story exists; return a note rather than manufacturing a feature if it does not.
```

## Stop conditions

Stop and report a bounded evidence gap when:
- the governing idea depends on a quotation that cannot be authenticated
- conflicting chronologies cannot be resolved
- the strongest reading requires invented motive or biography
- the subject yields facts but no consequential story
- the piece duplicates an existing Shakedown feature without a genuinely new argument

A thin subject is allowed to remain a note. Do not manufacture significance.
